# Reflection Note
**Hello dear reader!**


By developing this project, I learned how to interpret every problem
and actually find out on my own as SWEs do - debug and find solutions on their own by searching and reading others' code.
The thing is when you actually find out the solution on your own, you also learn how to adapt other code into your own codebase -
and this is a great way of learning. Because when you first solve the problem, it becomes easier to adapt and solve other problems,
and you interpret problem-solving in a different way, looking at problems from very different aspects.

You have no choice but to actually understand each line of code - which is where every developer should be.
The reason I work this way is that I want to have that desired knowledge that seniors have today, where
they found out the solutions on their own and got excellent at it.

So this project, is built the old way. YouTube videos, failing over and over again,
Stack Overflow, documentation, links, other repositories. No AI-generated code. Just figuring it out.
And that is the whole point.

I have never learned so much as I do now, because every time I solve a specific problem
I get that "Aha, it makes so much sense now" moment. This also makes developing really fun, and the learning curve
is huge, because of doing this on my own with the documentation of Spring, Docker, and everything else useful to this project.

I've come to an understanding that LLMs are a good tool, but that tool is useless for me as a developer if I don't actually
understand the whole output of the code each time. When you cannot trace line by line yourself, you cannot know how to implement features or understand memory allocation nor data structures.
It's like driving a manual car but not knowing when or how to switch gears at the right time, and when you don't, the car will stall and stop. In other words, the person cannot develop.

---

**Thank you for taking your time reading this.**

**I hope this letter can motivate someone who finds themselves in the same position.**
**You have to go through the same process that senior developers with years of experience went through before you. That is why they are where they are today.**


**If you truly have a burning desire to develop - there are absolutely no shortcuts.**
**Sometimes you just have to do things the old way, in order to become good enough to adapt the new ways with a positive outcome.**

---

a "You should do this or this is right, or wrong", but just purely based on what I felt in that time being stuck, and not learning as I should do.*
